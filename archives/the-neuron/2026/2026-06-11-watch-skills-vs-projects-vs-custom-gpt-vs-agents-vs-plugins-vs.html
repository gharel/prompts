See, AI has given us a new kind of productivity problem: five, six, maybe even seven different ways to customize our work with agents, three different words for “workflow,” and one very reasonable question: Which one am I actually supposed to use, when?

Today on Neuron LIVE, we’re sorting the whole mess: AI Skills, Projects, Gems, Custom GPTs, Agents, plugins, and maybe even “loops,” because apparently the AI industry looked at “agents” and thought, “Could use one more term.”

The goal is simple: by the end, you should know when to organize work, when to customize an assistant, when to save a reusable workflow, and when to let an AI system actually go do something.

This is basically the guide we wish existed before every AI lab dumped all these confusing terms on us.

What we’re testing live, on stream:

  • Projects (ChatGPT, Claude): when you need a place to organize ongoing work, context, files, instructions, and repeat tasks.

  • Gems & Custom GPTs (Gemini Gems, Custom GPTs): when you want a reusable custom assistant for a specific job.

  • Skills (OpenAI, Codex, Claude, Gemini ADK): when you want repeatable instructions, workflows, references, scripts, and process memory the AI can load when needed.

  • Agents (ChatGPT Agent, OpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Code agents, Google ADK): when you want AI to take actions on your behalf autonomously towards a goal.

  • Plugins (Codex plugins, ChatGPT Apps SDK, Claude plugins, Google ADK plugins): because yes, those are somehow back in the conversation too.

  • Loops (Addy Osmani’s loop engineering post, Claude dynamic workflows, Google ADK loop workflows): if we have time, we’ll touch the trendiest topic in AI discourse without making everyone pretend they already know what it means.

🎙️ In Case You Missed It… Part Two: Tiago Sada Explains Why the Internet Needs Proof of Human

Corey sat down with Tiago Sada, Chief Product Officer at Tools for Humanity, for a very timely conversation about one of the internet’s fastest-growing problems: proving there’s an actual human on the other side of the screen.

Tools for Humanity is building the technology behind World and World ID, a system designed to verify that someone is a real, unique person without making them hand over their full identity to every app, website, ticketing platform, dating app, or suspicious “free iPad” pop-up they encounter online.

TL;DW: Corey and Tiago dug into why CAPTCHAs, KYC checks, and AI detection are starting to crack under the pressure of bots, deepfakes, and AI agents. They also covered how World ID works, what it stores, what it deletes, and why “proof of human” could become a trust layer for the internet.

Why you should watch: The biggest unlock here is not just stopping bots from hoarding concert tickets like tiny digital goblins. It’s the bigger question: how do we let AI agents act on our behalf without letting fake humans, fake accounts, and fake identities wreck the internet in the process?

P.S. This gets surprisingly practical fast. Dating apps, Zoom calls, gaming, Shopify stores, ticketing, enterprise security,basically anywhere you currently wonder, “Is this person real?” World ID wants to be part of the answer.
