Apple’s AI era starts now?

We’re going LIVE right now, now that Apple has wrapped its biggest event of the year, and this one is bigger than yet another iPhone launch.

It’s John Ternus’s first major keynote as Apple CEO, and the question we care about is simple: does Apple finally look like an AI company?

We’ll break down what Apple actually showed around Siri and Apple Intelligence, what feels different under Ternus, and whether Apple is finally closing the gap with the rest of the AI industry (lol… ).

The AI bits we’re digging into:

  • Siri AI is not fully “done” yet: the English beta starts with iOS 27 on September 14, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish follow in October, and Apple hasn’t given a non-beta release date.

  • iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max go up for preorder September 12 and become available September 18. A20 Pro moves to 2nm and 32 neural-processing cores, pushing more advanced AI work directly onto the phone.

  • iPhone Duo arrives later: preorders open October 16, with availability starting October 23. Apple also said it used AI in manufacturing to tune the hinge components so they work together precisely.

  • Apple is pairing AI creation with provenance: iPhone 18 Pro supports SynthID plus a new Apple Reference Image system designed to help verify what the camera actually captured.

  • Some server-heavy Siri features will have usage limits, with increased access through eligible iCloud+ plans.

In other words: Apple’s AI pitch is finally moving from keynote promise to something we can actually use.

Then the rest of today’s AI news gets weird fast

  • Meta Muse: Meta launched a personal AI agent with a massive free usage tier, potentially up to 100M tokens per week.

  • OpenAI’s $1M math fight: OpenAI says a huge AI effort made progress on Navier-Stokes, one of math’s Millennium Prize problems, and immediately sparked a fight over credit.

  • AI risk: An Anthropic scientist put the chance of AI causing human extinction above 10%.

  • Microsoft’s monster patch: Microsoft fixed a record 972 vulnerabilities, including 112 rated critical.

  • U.S. agencies vs. Chinese AI labs: the NSA, FBI, and CISA warned that companies including DeepSeek, Moonshot, and Alibaba are distilling Western AI systems “at an industrial scale.”

And yes, Sam Altman is apparently very excited about an Ocarina of Time remake. Important work continues.
