AWS Startup Advisor brings architecture, security, cost, and scaling guidance into Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, VS Code, and Kiro.

Welcome, humans.

Entrepreneurs and builders, this one’s for you.

AI can now help a nontechnical founder turn an idea into a working prototype before bed.

The uncomfortable part comes the next morning, when that prototype needs authentication, security, reliable infrastructure, manageable cloud bills, and enough resilience to survive actual contact with paying customers.

In our latest podcast episode, Corey and Grant spoke with Deap Ubhi, Global Director of Technology for Startups at AWS, about the new dividing line between making software and building something people can safely depend on.

Deap has built several companies himself. His biggest observation about the AI era is simple: startup iteration cycles have collapsed from months and quarters into hours and days.

A founder can now build a prototype, hand it to prospective customers, collect feedback, and ship the next version while the original idea is still warm.

The bad news is that you can also build 15 side projects and accidentally turn your life into a browser tab.

Here’s our favorite parts:

  • (05:54) Your startup suddenly gets more lottery tickets: Faster iteration gives founders many more attempts to discover what customers actually want before their runway disappears.

  • (09:34) When the fun prototype becomes a real business: Deap explains why security, resilience, reliability, and availability become unavoidable once customers depend on the product.

  • (13:07) Find out whether the product stinks before hiring everyone: AI lets founders validate demand before spending heavily on engineers and infrastructure.

  • (15:27) The new founder problem is too many possibilities: When building gets easier, focus and product taste become more valuable, not less.

  • (20:22) The strongest use case may happen before you code: Startup Advisor can help a first-time builder plan an AWS architecture without already knowing the names of 200-plus services.

The episode’s most useful idea is that AI does not eliminate technical judgment. It changes when you need it.

You can test the product before making a major technical investment. Once the signal is real, AWS Startup Advisor can help surface the production questions a first-time builder may not know to ask.

Why watch this? Because this conversation shows how to move from “Claude made something that works on my laptop” to software with a credible plan for authentication, infrastructure, security, costs, and scale.

Startup Advisor works inside tools including Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, VS Code, and Kiro, so builders can call on AWS guidance while planning or coding instead of opening a separate consulting project.

How to add an AWS expert to your coding workflow

AWS Startup Advisor is an agent plugin that packages guidance from AWS specialists into the coding environments founders already use.

Deap described it as a little AWS CTO sitting on your shoulder. You can call it when you need help with a specific task, but its more valuable role may be identifying decisions you did not realize existed.

For example, a first-time founder could explain:

  • What they are building

  • Who will use it

  • How customers will access it

  • Whether it handles sensitive information

  • What stage the product has reached

Startup Advisor can then help plan the architecture, flag security considerations, recommend AWS services, and guide the implementation.

You do not need to keep it involved in every line of code. Invoke it when planning a feature, adding authentication, creating infrastructure, reviewing security, preparing to deploy, or wondering whether the current setup can scale.

Install it where you build

  • Web: Join AWS Activate

  • Claude Code: Install the AWS Startup Advisor plugin

  • VS Code: Open the Visual Studio Marketplace listing

  • Cursor: Open the Open VSX listing

  • Kiro: Open the Open VSX listing

A useful first prompt might look like this:

I’m building [describe the product] for [describe the customer]. Users will access it through [web, mobile, API, etc.], and it will handle [describe the data]. I currently have [prototype, early users, paying customers, etc.]. Help me plan the AWS architecture, security, authentication, observability, deployment, and cost considerations I should address at this stage. Separate what I need now from what can wait until later.

The goal is not to build enterprise infrastructure before anyone wants the product. It is to know which shortcuts are harmless experiments and which ones become expensive traps.
