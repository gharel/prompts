😺 Walmart and Amazon disagree on who should do your shopping

You’re ready to let AI do your shopping. Now the stores want a say in which AI gets your money. Apparently, even your robot has to get past the bouncer.

Walmart is making room for external agents, with separate profiles, purchase records, and spending limits for each agent. Taking the opposite approach, Amazon has pushed back against third-party agents while promoting its own shopping assistant, Alexa for Shopping.

These outside shoppers include Meta’s Muse and Anthropic’s Claude Duet, who can make purchases without your approval. Perplexity’s Comet shows where this gets messy: Amazon asked it to stop offering Amazon shopping, citing problems with the shopping experience and customer service.

Walmart’s Agent Profiles are due to launch within the next month. Here’s what handing your shopping list to an agent would actually look like:

  • Make it ask before every purchase, or give it a spending limit so it can handle the small stuff.

  • Even under budget, it may still need your OK. Walmart can require another approval.

  • Let it buy without handing over your card details. It can’t change your payment methods, and you can delete its profile whenever you want.

There are reasons to set those boundaries. Six banks, including Bank of America and NatWest, warned last month that shopping agents might enter card details directly into websites or steer customers toward payment methods with weaker protections. Customers could be unsure who to turn to if something goes wrong.

Buying through an outside assistant is already possible in some places. Instacart’s ChatGPT integration turns a recipe request into a grocery cart you can edit and pay for inside the conversation.

Why it matters: Your AI could soon shop around for the best deals, but retailers would rather keep it on a short leash. Walmart is opening the door to outside agents, while Amazon prefers its own. The big question: Will your AI work for you, or become the world's most sophisticated store greeter?

I wanted AI to handle my shopping, not get caught in a corporate love triangle with Walmart, Amazon, and my credit card.

🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Check the story before AI builds your slides

You ask AI for a presentation. It gives you polished slides, then you realize the recommendation is buried on slide twelve. Lovely font. Wrong story.

Gemini Notebook’s new review feature lets you inspect the outline and give written feedback before it generates the deck. That gives you a chance to fix the argument before getting distracted by the visuals.

Try this:

  1. Add your sources, choose Slide Deck in the Studio panel, and turn on Review outline.

  2. Check whether the recommendation arrives early enough, whether the evidence supports it, and whether anything essential is missing.

  3. Give specific feedback before generating. For example, ask it to move the recommendation ahead of the background and put the supporting evidence immediately afterward.

Making a video instead? Use Review producer notes for the same checkpoint. The feature is rolling out gradually, so it may not appear yet.

Fix the story while it’s still an outline. The fonts can wait.
