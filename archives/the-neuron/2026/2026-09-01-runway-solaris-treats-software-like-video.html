😺 Runway’s Solaris generates software like video

Most AI coding tools write code first. Your browser then turns that code into the buttons, menus, and screens you see.

Runway’s Solaris is trying a stranger route. It generates the interface itself, one frame at a time, as you interact with it.

Here's what happened:

  • Runway introduced Solaris, its first “Interface World Model,” built on Gen-4.5.

  • In plain English, Solaris predicts what the next screen should look like after each click or drag instead of writing code first.

  • Runway says 250 evaluators preferred Solaris over Claude Opus 5-coded UIs in 61% of instruction-following and 71% of natural-behavior comparisons.

  • Early access is request-only for now.

Our take: The weird part is that Solaris makes the model itself responsible for what you see next. Developers would not need to prebuild every button, menu, or screen state because the interface can be generated on demand.

That flexibility comes with a new reliability problem. A button still has to act like the same button five clicks later, and accessibility, security, saved state, and recovery still have to work every time.

Ben South has argued future software interfaces could be continuously generated, meaning a model redraws the interface on demand instead of assembling fixed components. Solaris is the clearest demo yet.

The next test is boring real work, where consistency matters more than magic.

🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Make AI report only what changed

Ever ask AI for a project update and get the entire history back? If your team uses Asana, ChatGPT can now read tasks, comments, owners, activity notes, and due dates through the Asana app.

That means you can ask for only what changed: new blockers, overdue work, owner changes, deadline shifts, and decisions somebody needs to make.

  1. Connect Asana from ChatGPT’s app directory or Settings > Apps.

  2. Give it a time window, then name the changes you care about.

  3. Ask it to cite the task or comment behind each update. Review everything before letting AI change anything.

Prompt:

Review my Asana projects using recent task comments, activity notes, assignees, and due dates. Report only what changed since [DATE]: new blockers, newly overdue work, owner changes, due-date changes, and decisions someone needs to make. For each item, name the task and the source comment or activity note that supports it. Do not create or edit anything.

New from The Neuron: AI’s $11T compute bet

Okay, about that $11T number: bulls and bears are looking at the same spending boom and reaching completely different conclusions.

Our full breakdown covers whether demand can justify the buildout, whether subsidies and deal structures are masking weak economics, and the stranger risk that AI runs short of compute instead of drowning in it.

Want the raw arguments?

  • Gavin Baker argues AI demand is still badly underestimated, so even a huge buildout can make economic sense despite bubble risk from debt-fueled financing.

  • Dylan Patel + Dwarkesh Patel argue OpenAI and Anthropic could absorb roughly half of new AI compute, then turn that capacity into a huge share of digital work… but there’s a lot of physical bottlenecks in the middle.

  • Ed Zitron argues subsidies and companies funding one another’s AI spending can make demand look healthier than it is… and the sticker shock from AI’s full price might pop the bubble in today’s capital-constrained market.
