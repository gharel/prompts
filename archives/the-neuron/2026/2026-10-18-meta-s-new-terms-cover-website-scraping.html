😺 Meta's new terms could let it scrape your website

You use Instagram to bring people to your work. Now, the fine print could give Meta permission to collect that work for itself. That’s quite a referral fee.

Here’s what changed: Meta’s business terms take effect October 30 and authorize scraping websites associated with businesses using its products. That means automatically collecting content from those sites to develop and train Meta products. Legal experts told Nieman Lab that could include training its AI on publishers’ articles.

This could reach beyond what you post on Instagram. Sharing an article on Instagram could authorize Meta to collect the full article from your site, under the experts’ reading.

You can’t reject these terms separately and keep using Meta’s business products, according to Nieman. That’s a lot hiding behind “keep using our services.”

Meta says the update is about ads and recommendations. They say it’s about simplifying existing rules for collecting product information, not claiming a new right to scrape news. But experts question how broadly the terms could be used.

Even if Meta gets permission to collect content, using it to train AI is another legal question: does it have the right to learn from that work? Five major publishers sued Meta in May, alleging it pirated millions of works to train its Llama AI models. That’s a separate case, but it shows why a new set of terms doesn’t end the argument over whose work AI can learn from.

Now put that next to what other companies are doing with the same content:

  • Google is paying publishers. On October 15, it said more than 400 publications participate in its News AI pilot. CNN, Axios, and the Financial Times are among the new partners. Their content powers things like article summaries and audio briefings.

  • Cloudflare is testing payment for AI use. Its Pay Per Use beta, announced September 30, lets publishers accept offers from AI companies. The buyer sets the price and what it wants to use the content for, then reports its own usage. Cloudflare handles the bills and payouts.

Why this matters: If you use Instagram or Facebook to promote your business, Meta's new terms could have implications for content you never actually posted there. And with AI companies increasingly hungry for training data, the bigger question is who gets to decide how your work is used.

Apparently, having an Instagram account now comes with a side quest in copyright law.

🎓 AI Skill of the Day: Turn your open tabs into a comparison table

You've got five browser tabs open, each showing a different product, pricing plan, or research article. And, naturally, every website organizes its information differently.

Congratulations. Your browser is now a homework assignment.

ChatGPT's Chrome extension can help you compare information across open tabs without constantly switching between them.

Here's how to try it:

  1. Open three to five pages you want to compare, such as software pricing plans or competing products.

  2. Install the ChatGPT Chrome extension and connect it to the ChatGPT desktop app.

  3. In a supported desktop session, reference your Chrome tabs with @Chrome and ask:

Try this prompt:

Compare the pages in my open tabs. Create a table showing key differences, pricing, pros and cons, and anything important that's missing. Include source links and flag anything you can't verify.

For pricing comparisons, ask ChatGPT to distinguish monthly payments from annual commitments and identify features that cost extra.

You can also ask it to organize your tabs by topic when you're done.

Five tabs enter. One useful table leaves.

Note: Browser integration availability may depend on your app version, plan, and workspace settings.
