Let’s talk about Grok—the world’s new top AI?

On Monday, Elon livestreamed the announcement of Grok 3, xAI’s latest chatbot. And according to all the “early” benchmarks, the thing is a beast:

  • xAI says it outperforms Gemini-2 Pro, DeepSeek V3, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and GPT 4o.

  • Has a “Think Mode” for reasoning and “Big Brain Mode” for harder problems.

  • Includes DeepSearch—Grok’s version of “Deep Research.”

  • There’s a mini version, API coming later, and eventually, it’ll get open-sourced.

It turns out that the mystery chatbot in the LLM Arena named Chocolate was in fact “Early Grok-3”. And according to “early voting”, it’s now ranked #1 in the arena. 

Here’s some early takes (mostly from X users): 

  • Grok 3 isn’t as consistent as o3-mini and Claude 3.5 Sonnet at coding (part 2).

  • Grok 3 IS good at video games—here’s Portal 2, and here’s Mario lite. 

  • Andrej Karpathy thinks it's equal to ChatGPT o1 Pro (which costs $200 a month).

Many people think Grok 3 is particularly impressive (thanks to Elon’s Colossus supercomputer) considering how late in the game xAI started.   

Also… to whoever made this… you’re out of control. 

Now here’s the problem: Many people don’t trust Elon. We were shocked to find a lot of people don’t even trust Google (call it “the Spite Coefficient”). So even if xAI released the best performing AI model of all time (as it claims to be), the likelihood of mass adoption would be somewhat curbed (by the spite).

Not only that: right now, to use Grok 3, you have to be an X Premium+ subscriber (for $40 a month, or $32.92 if billed annually). That might be $160 a month less than o1 Pro, but it’s still a lot.

Here’s why “open source” matters: DeepSeek as a competitor to OpenAI can be seen as also deeply untrustworthy; it’s failed multiple safety tests, its app has an overly invasive privacy policy (it’ll take whatever data you give it), and it’s a Chinese model that launched in the middle of an AI turf war between China and the US. 

All that doesn’t matter for DeepSeek R1’s adoption—because it's open source. Anyone can take the code, run it on their machine, and even if it doesn’t perform as well as the cloud-hosted version, can still safely host it locally and offline. 

If Elon delivers on his promise that Grok 3 will eventually be open-source, then Grok might have a chance. But if people still don’t use Gemini because they can’t trust Google… It's gonna be an uphill battle.
