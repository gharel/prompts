NEWS BRIEF: Here’s Everything to Know About ChatGPT Pulse

OpenAI just flipped the script on how we interact with ChatGPT. Instead of you constantly prompting it for answers, it just launched Pulse, a new feature that wakes up every morning with personalized research it did for you overnight.

Think of it as your own AI research assistant who preps a daily briefing just for you (kinda like us newsletters! Sure, it’ll be customized just for you, but it won’t provide witty sardonic asides like this one… so you won’t replace us, right?? RIGHT?!).

Here’s how to make Pulse actually work for you:

  • Don't be passive. Early tester Olivia Moore (of A16z) found the real magic happens when you actively tell Pulse what you want. Use the “curate” button to give it specific instructions like, “focus on professional tennis updates,” or “find next steps for my triathlon training.”

  • Connect your apps. For the best results, you can connect your Gmail and Google Calendar. This allows Pulse to do things like draft a meeting agenda or remind you to buy a birthday gift. It's off by default, so you have to opt-in.

  • Give it feedback. Use the thumbs up/down on each card. OpenAI says this feedback only trains your version of Pulse, making it more useful over time.

The bigger picture is clear. This is OpenAI's play to become your first click of the day, competing directly with news apps and social media for that prime morning real estate. Uniquely, it’s a finite feed, designed to end, not for doom-scrolling; which conveniently also creates a perfect surface for future ads. Insiders have reported OpenAI is exploring ad-leadership hires, and Pulse looks like the ideal place to start.

This move also comes at the same time Meta launched Vibes, a new feed of AI-generated videos. Together, they signal the next big thing in AI: the “feed.” As Sam Altman and others have intimated, the “chat” form factor for AI is saturated. So now “the feed” form factor has begun in earnest.

But where Vibes is a public, creative, TikTok-style social feed, Pulse is a private, productive, anti-social feed designed to help you (not to get you scrolling).

Prompt Tip of the Day

Ryan Carr of Moodboard (a “vibe marketing” newsletter) just shared the ultimate subject line writer prompt, and TBH, it’s quite simple but effective. If you opened today’s email, you can thank Ryan! So if it worked on you, it’ll probably work FOR you. The whole thing is too long to include, but we’ll give a TL;DR of Ryan’s process:

  • The AI prompt that generates 8 different subject line options using proven psychological triggers.

  • It uses 8 Strategies: Curiosity gap (this is the big one), benefit-focused, urgency, question-based, data-driven, contrarian, story-based, direct statement

  • His guidelines: Keep it under 50 characters, avoid spam words, use power words, be conversational not corporate.

The key Insight: “Prompt for options”: instead of accepting one AI output, have it give you multiple angles to choose from (good practice for all prompting, btw).

Here’s our recommendation on how to implement this:

  • Step 1: Copy the prompt template from the article into the instructions of a project folder / gem on Gemini / customGPT (project folder is simplest!).

  • Step 2: Replace where it says “[Paste your newsletter/email content here]” with “the content to use will be pasted attached in the context window.”

  • Step 3: Paste the full text of your email content in the context window and hit enter.

  • Step 4: Pick your favorites, or ask for more and give it notes on what you want more / less of.
