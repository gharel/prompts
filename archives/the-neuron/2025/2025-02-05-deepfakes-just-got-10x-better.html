Deepfakes are about to get out of control…like 10x better out of control.

Ever wished you could make that old photo of your grandma sing “Happy Birthday”? Or turn your LinkedIn headshot into a TED talk? Or, IDK, create a realistic version of yourself to attend Zoom meetings while you’re sipping Mai Tai’s in Honolulu?

Well, ByteDance (TikTok's parent company)’s new AI model can do exactly that, and TBH, it’s sorta terrifying.

It’s called OmniHuman-1, and it takes any photo—selfies, full-body shots, even cartoons—and makes it move realistically based on both audio or video inputs. And, unlike previous models, it works with pretty much any kind of image (paper here).

Here are 10 incredible examples—can you guess which is our favorite? Shockingly, it’s NOT this clip of Taylor Swift singing a Naruto theme song, or THIS clip of Jensen Huang rapping…but that was a close second.

What makes OmniHuman-1 special is its versatility:

  • Creates eerily realistic gestures and expressions.

  • Handles both singing and talking naturally.

  • Even works with non-human objects.

Think of it like a universal translator between still images and motion. You feed it a photo and some audio, and it figures out how to make that photo move naturally with the sound.

See, China isn’t just leading the open-source AI race for reasoning models… with Tuesday’s release of OmniHuman-1, and previous leading video models like Hunyuan, Kling, and MiniMax, it’s clear the country is heavily focused on video content, too

And this is just one piece of the synthetic media explosion:

NVIDIA-backed Synthesia just raised a new round of $180M that values the company at $2.1B and now has 60K customers. It and HeyGen (who raised $60M) are the two leading avatar startups, and for good reason—they look good.

Gone are the days of wonky static avatars; these tools now provide motion control and other features that make their avatars more realistic than ever.

Synthesia gets its realistic voices from ElevenLabs, the leading voice AI who just raised its own round of $180M at a valuation of $3.3B. There’s also Cartesia, who makes an incredibly life-like voice AI called Sonic (demo here).

All of these companies want to become more lifelike. You look at their roadmaps, and that’s basically what’s coming—moving AI from the uncanny valley to the make money valley.

The problem? The most profitable use for life-like avatars is deepfakes, either used to influence elections or scam people and corporations out of billions. And if scammers get access to tools like OmniHuman-1 to play with? I think Darth Vader said it best…
