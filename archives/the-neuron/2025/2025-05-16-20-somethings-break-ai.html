Sam Altman Unveils How Young People Use AI

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman just dropped some serious insights about how younger generations are using AI—and it's way more sophisticated than your typical Google search.

During a recent talk at Sequoia Capital, Altman revealed that young people aren't just using AI tools; they're basically treating them like a life operating system. Move over, smartphone. There's a new digital assistant in town.

Here's the wild part: While older folks might use ChatGPT as a glorified Google replacement, people in their 20s and 30s are using AI as an actual life advisor. We're talking about a digital confidant that knows the full context of their life.

Altman shared some mind-blowing observations:

  • Young people are memorizing complex prompts like they're cheat codes.

  • They're setting up intricate AI systems that connect to multiple files.

  • Most shockingly? They don't make life decisions without consulting ChatGPT.

That’s right. Young users have developed sophisticated AI workflows:

  • Connecting multiple data sources.

  • Creating complex prompt libraries.

  • Using AI as a contextual advisor that understands their entire social ecosystem.

It's like having a super-intelligent friend who knows everything about your life, can analyze complex situations, and offers personalized advice—all without judgment.

Altman compared this to the smartphone revolution. Remember how younger people instantly got touchscreen tech while older generations struggled? We're seeing the same pattern with AI.

If you want to understand the future of AI, watch how 20-year-olds are using it.

Our take: AI isn't just a tool for these young users—it's becoming an extension of their decision-making process. Whether you find that exciting or slightly terrifying probably depends on your age.

Want to level up your OWN AI game? Start thinking like a 20-year-old: complex prompts, multiple data sources, and AI as your personal advisor. This is a slightly older video (from last September) but it’s a GREAT resource for learning how to use Claude like a 20-y/o.

Here’s the most useful parts for implementing Sam’s advice:

  • Creating Projects (47:23): Learn to set up specialized workspaces in Claude for different life areas like the digital natives Sam Altman described.

  • Custom Instructions (49:00): Master persistent instructions that shape Claude's responses—the "complex prompts" young people memorize.

  • Project Knowledge (52:04): Upload multiple references (up to 500 pages) to give Claude comprehensive context about your life and decisions.

  • Combining Features (57:23): Build sophisticated AI systems by connecting instructions, shortcuts, and knowledge bases into a contextual advisor.

By creating projects with custom instructions and connecting multiple data sources, you can transform Claude from a simple search tool into a personal advisor that understands your full context—just like the tech-savvy 20-somethings who wouldn't make the decision to watch the above video or not without consulting their AI first.
