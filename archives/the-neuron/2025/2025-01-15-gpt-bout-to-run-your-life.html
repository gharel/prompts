ChatGPT just got a personal assistant (and soon, everything else will too)

Yesterday, OpenAI quietly released it’s first “agent-adjacent” feature: Tasks.

Tasks is basically a to-do list that can schedule future actions—think weekly news briefings, daily workout plans, or just reminding you to call Mom next Tuesday (available for Plus, Pro, and Teams users with broader access coming soon). Don’t give me that look—you know you forget too sometimes!

OpenAI isn't the only one making AI more... well, assistant-y. Late last year, Google launched Agentspace, a platform that lets companies build their own AI agents.

Unlike basic chatbots, these agents can search across your company's entire data ecosystem (email, docs, Jira—you name it), plus the internet in real-time. They're already rolling out specialized versions for retail, helping stores manage inventory and customer service through AI.

And don't forget Amazon—so far they've spent two years secretly rebuilding Alexa to be more ChatGPT-like. The holdup? Those pesky AI “hallucinations.”

Speaking of hallucinations (when AI confidently makes stuff up), here's where the industry stands as of December 2024:

From Vectara’s hallucination leaderboard

  • Google's Gemini Flash leads the pack with a 1.3% hallucination rate.

  • OpenAI's GPT-4 family follows close behind at 1.5-1.8%.

  • Interestingly, Claude was not on the list…what gives? 

Now, these percentages might seem tiny, but even “small” error rates can cause big problems.

Take researcher Anj Simmons' recent findings: When asked about a mountain's height, GPT-4 was 99.3% likely to give the correct answer in isolation. But add some unrelated context? That accuracy plummeted to 32.1%.

This matters more than ever as AI moves from “chat companion” to “thing that runs your life.” Amazon's Alexa team knows this firsthand—the reason AlexaGPT is taking so long is because they're struggling to get hallucinations “close to zero” (while handling hundreds of third-party integrations at speed) before they launch it.

As one Amazon insider put it: when you're processing billions of requests weekly, even a tiny error rate means thousands of mistakes daily.

That’s why Anj Simmons thinks the biggest AI risk rn isn't superintelligent AI taking over—it's us rushing to integrate AI into critical systems before it's ready.

So while having ChatGPT remind you about meetings is great… maybe hold off on having it manage your medication schedule just yet.
