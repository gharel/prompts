Tens of billions invested in Healthcare AI so far... and your hospital probably can't use any of it

Tried finding a new doctor lately? Every call ends the same way: “Sorry, we're not accepting new patients” or “Sure, we can see you... in 4 months!”

It's exactly these kinds of headaches that have investors pour BILLIONS into healthcare in 2024—we’re talking $10.5B across 511 deals—and if January is any indication, 2025 is on pace to pass that.

Let's see who's working on fixing our broken healthcare system...

  • Qventus snagged $105M for their AI “operational assistants” that help hospitals run smoother. Think less paperwork, more patient care—they've already eliminated 36,000+ excess hospital days per year.

  • Hippocratic AI hit unicorn status by creating AI agents for stuff like pre-op prep and appointment scheduling—they've already signed 23 contracts with major health systems.

  • Innovaccer grabbed $275M to become the data plumbers of healthcare— connecting all those siloed systems and adding AI co-pilots for things like medical scribing and insurance approvals.

Turns out, “AI scribe” tools in particular have become so common, the startups making them are coming up with new ways to brand themselves to stand out.

Medical payment software companies are getting in on AI. too. WayStar, which processed $1.2T in payments, just released new genAI tools to automate provider claim appeals faster than a Zyn-pack popping paralegal can say “reimbursement.”

AI in medical technology is sort of a different story.

  • Bright side: the FDA has approved 500+ AI medical devices.

  • Not so bright side: of the 16 medical AI procedures tracked in insurance claims, only 4 have been used more than 1,000 times.

  • Mid (??) side: Most haven't even been around a year (median age: ~374 days)… so there's clearly a gap between hype and real-world usage.

Now, where is this AI med-tech actually showing up? Exactly where you'd expect:

  • 71% of academic medical centers use AI vs just 9% of other hospitals.

  • Rich zip codes have 2x the adoption rate of poorer areas.

  • Medicare pays $45 for an AI eye scan while private insurance pays $127.

Why this matters: While VCs are betting billions that AI will fix healthcare's problems, 2025 will be about the unglamorous work of getting doctors to actually use these tools. There’s no industry standard atm, and HHS only yesterday released “strategic plan” for how to address AI in healthcare.

With nearly half of US doctors reporting burnout, doctors are optimistic that AI can help. The willingness is there, so the opportunity is huge.

And there are a ton of cool ideas for where AI-powered health-tech can go from here. But first, can y’all help us “undoctored” folks actually book that annual check-up we’ve been putting off?
