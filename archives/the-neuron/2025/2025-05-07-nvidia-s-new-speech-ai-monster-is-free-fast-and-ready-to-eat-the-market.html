Welcome, humans.

We’ve entered the era of emotionally manipulative autocomplete. Language models (LLMs) are now serving you praise with a side of product placement. And it’s more than just annoying. It’s engineered.

They’re called LLM “dark patterns.” These sneaky UX tricks are designed to steer you into actions you didn’t really choose—like buying, subscribing, or sharing—whether you meant to or not. They’re like UX dark patterns, but now they talk back.

Think confirm-shaming, fake urgency, and sneaky unsub buttons… except this time, the guilt trip comes with a compliment: “You’re a genius for buying that $300 serum.”

NVIDIA just dropped a speech AI nuke… and gave it away for free…

NVIDIA’s latest flex? Whisper-level speech recognition… but open-source, commercial-ready, and deployable on devices with less RAM than your smart toaster.

To those of you new to AI, all that means you can run this new AI that you can talk to locally on your computer and not on the cloud—for free.

Meet Parakeet 2, officially known as Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-V2 (like R2D2, but doesn’t roll off the tongue). It’s the second-gen automatic speech recognition model from NVIDIA, and it’s absurdly fast. Like, “transcribe an hour of audio in a second” fast, according to Hugging Face’s Vaibhav “VB” Srivastav.

Big up to NVIDIA for introducing us to what is now our second favorite Parakeet—after Paulie, of course.

And this bird’s not just fast—it’s good. Parakeet 2 just flew to the top of Hugging Face’s Open ASR Leaderboard with a Word Error Rate of 6.05%. That beats out heavyweights like Microsoft’s Phi-4 and ElevenLabs’ Scribe.

Also, because it’s fully open-source under the permissive CC-BY-4.0 license, you can use, tweak, and sell what you build with it—no strings attached.

Try that with Eleven Labs, whose prices have become bloated due to the pricing power its enjoyed for being the top voice model for far too long…

What’s under the hood?

  • Trained on NVIDIA’s Granary dataset: 120K hours of transcribed and pseudo-labeled English audio (LibriSpeech, Common Voice, YouTube-Commons, Librilight).

  • Handles punctuation, capitalization, and word-level timestamps out of the box.

  • Can run on systems with just 2GB of RAM, which makes it edge-device friendly—even if you’re not rocking a DGX box.

  • NVIDIA’s opening up the dataset post-Interspeech 2025, adding more transparency to the pipeline.

Why it matters: Parakeet 2 is a strategic missile aimed straight at the heart of closed speech ecosystems:

  • Fast and accurate enough to rival the best.

  • Lightweight enough for edge deployments.

  • Free for commercial use.

  • Compliant with NVIDIA’s responsible AI practices (no personal data, full documentation, partial bias mitigation).

Our take: NVIDIA is planting flags beyond GPUs—building a sticky software stack (like CUDA, but for every AI vertical) that developers, startups, and researchers won’t want to leave. If you’re building transcription tools, voice assistants, or real-time subtitles, this is the part where you ask yourself:

Why would I pay for a speech model… when NVIDIA just handed me one for free?

Prompt Tip of the Day

Ever feel like you're speaking alien to your AI? The “Triple-Vision Translator” hack unlocks clarity at every level. Ask ChatGPT or Claude to explain any complex concept three different ways—for a six grader (or kindergartener, if you want a really simple version), a college student, and a domain expert.

Just copy-paste: “Explain [complex concept] three times: (a) to a 12-year-old (b) to a college student (c) to a domain expert who wants edge-case caveats”
