Eric Schmidt thinks AI is underhyped (yeah, we’re not joking)…

Some of you (let’s just say our more spicy commenters) will occasionally leave us feedback that we here at The Neuron over-hype AI and AI related news. But according to Eric Schmidt, the former CEO of GOogle, we're actually underestimating the transformative power of artificial intelligence.

Remember when AlphaGo invented a Go move no human had ever seen? Schmidt says that was just the beginning. He sees AI as a revolutionary force that could reshape everything from business to scientific discovery.

Here’s what he said at a recent TED Talk: 

  • AI is moving beyond language into strategic planning and complex problem-solving.

  • Computational requirements for AI are skyrocketing—potentially increasing by a factor of 100 to 1,000.

  • The ultimate goal: AI agents that can run entire business processes, communicating with each other in natural language.

See, Schmidt believes we're on the cusp of incredible breakthroughs:

  • Personalized tutors for every child in their native language.

  • AI healthcare assistants for underserved regions.

  • Potential solutions to complex scientific mysteries like dark energy.

  • Reach a projected 30% annual productivity increase that economists can't even model.

In sum Schmidt believes the arrival of AI—from general intelligence to superintelligence—is the most significant development in 500 to 1,000 years of human society. And it's happening right now, in our lifetime. Lucky us!

The stakes are high, though. Schmidt warns of a potential AI “arms race” between the US and China, with both nations investing billions to lead the next technological frontier. Schmidt says the winner could gain unprecedented economic and strategic advantages (he even wrote this paper on his AI concerns; if you’re curious what it says, copy + paste it it into AI Studio and chat with it directly). .

Despite all that, Schmidt remains optimistic. His take? Humans aren't going anywhere. Lawyers, for example, will have more sophisticated lawsuits, and same for other jobs who will grow and scale in sophistication.

And if you’re worried about what this means for job security, Schmidt says the #1 issue AI solves is the lower birth rate of modern societies; we just don’t have enough humans today, and will continue to not have enough humans into the future, to do everything we need to do. AI helps solve that problem.

Quick, somebody tell all our bosses we humans are a hot commodity so they don’t fire us all!!
