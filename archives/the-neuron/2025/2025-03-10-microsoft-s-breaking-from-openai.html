Microsoft is about to release its own AI models to rival OpenAI, Anthropic, and Elon…

It’s been a minute since we dived into any Microsoft vs OpenAI drama, largely because OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s been busy with a new baby (congrats!) and a potential eye-popping fundraise from SoftBank.

How’s this for drama: The Information just published a fascinating exclusive about how Microsoft’s “AI CEO”, Mustafa Suleyman, has made significant progress with internal AI models called MAI that supposedly match OpenAI’s models on benchmarks.

Here’s the TL;DR:

  • Microsoft is swapping out OpenAI models for MAI models inside Copilot.

  • Microsoft will offer these MAI models directly to developers later this year.

  • Suleyman said “we are now using, under the hood, essentially all of the models from major labs.”

That last point means Microsoft has tested xAI’s Grok, Anthropic’s Claude, Meta’s Llama, and DeepSeek’s open source models in Copilot, too.

The article also spilled some tea on where Suleyman and Altman’s relationship soured—allegedly, when OpenAI wouldn’t reveal how o1’s “thinking” works on a tense phone call. So really, OpenAI started it yeah?

To us, this is all just more evidence of AI “models” becoming like software stacks—a layer or two deeper than most people care to know about. If that’s not true now, GPT-5 will make sure of that.

Right now, OpenAI has so many models, it just had to make a new page to make it easier to compare them all.

But to regular people chatting with ChatGPT or Copilot (and their bosses), it doesn’t really matter what model is running under the hood—as long as it gets the job done.

Which AI is best for the job, then? As far as the LM Arena is concerned, the answer is Grok 3, now free for anyone to try with Think mode (Grok's version of o1’s reasoning) and DeepSearch (Grok’s version of Deep Research). You get ~15 normal messages, ~10 thinking, and ~10 DeepSearch questions every 2 hours.

However, many people won't try Grok (even IF it's free) because they don't want to support Elon. They’ll stick with OpenAI, Claude, Copilot or Gemini instead.

This is why what really matters isn't what's under the hood, but what's on the surface—the brand, user experience, and most important of all, the distribution.

People who do use Grok like it because it's “elegantly integrated” inside of X.com. Google is about to embed a Perplexity-style “AI Mode” directly into search results (demo), and Meta can instantly distribute any new AI tools to a billion users on launch.

While ChatGPT recently doubled from 200M to 400M weekly users in just six months (175M on mobile), Copilot has its own powerful distribution channel—it's built right into the workflow of 2M companies who require their employees to use Microsoft 365.

But what if your Copilot (which you have to use for work) was actually running on Gemini, or… *~Le Gasp~* …Grok?! Would you even notice? Would you even care?
