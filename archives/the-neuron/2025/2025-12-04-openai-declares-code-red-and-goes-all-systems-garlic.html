OpenAI Declared a “Code Red”(But We Hear a Secret Model Called “Garlic” Might Save Them)

Remember when Google declared “code red” back in 2022 because ChatGPT was eating their lunch? Well, well, well… how the turntables have… tabled.

Quick refresher: So, a lot was made about Sam Altman’s recent “Code Red” memo in response to Google’s Gemini 3.0. This video probably does the best summary of the situation (and it’s hilarious AF).

It turns out, the memo requested the team freeze all non-essential projects like shopping agents, health tools, advertising agents, even their new personal assistant Pulse, and to focus everything on making ChatGPT better. Whatcha think Code Red means people!!

Was Gemini 3.0 really that big of a deal for OpenAI? Weeell…

  • Gemini 3 topped industry benchmarks when it launched last month, and users are loving it.

  • Not like he’s a big deal or anything, but Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff literally tweeted “I'm not going back” after trying it.

  • ChatGPT still has 800M weekly users compared to Gemini's 650M monthly users, but Google's ecosystem advantage (Search, Workspace, Gmail) is a monster. Though TBH, Google’s AI workspace integrations are probably the company’s #1 weak spot right now. Have you tried Gemini in Docs? It works about as well as “Apple Intelligence.” As in, it’s NOT intelligent.

Now here's the plot twist: OpenAI isn't just playing defense here. They're building a new model codenamed “Garlic” that's reportedly performing well against both Gemini 3 and Anthropic's Opus 4.5 in internal tests. We might see it drop as GPT-5.2 or GPT-5.5 early next year. From what we hear, this version’s still a little spicy, too… fitting for garlic!

Meanwhile, Anthropic is cashing in (or should we say, cashing out?): While OpenAI scrambles, Anthropic just announced a $200M partnership with Snowflake AND started prepping for an IPO that could value them at $300B+. They hired Wilson Sonsini (the law firm that took Google and LinkedIn public) and are targeting 2026 for a potential listing. Not financial advice, but based on our personal Claude usage… that’ll probably go quite well.

Speaking of getting spicy, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei told Andrew Ross Sorkin that while he's bullish on AI technology, he has concerns about the economic side of AI spending, noting that some industry players are “yoloing“ and taking unwise risks with their capital deployment.

  1. He explained that Anthropic faces a “cone of uncertainty“ in revenue forecasting: the company's revenue has grown 10x annually for three years (from zero to $100M in 2023, $100M to $1B in 2024, and is projected to reach $8-10B by the end of 2025). but the 1-2 year lag time required to build data centers means companies must commit capital now for uncertain future demand.

  2. He also said Anthropic manages this risk conservatively by planning for lower revenue scenarios and maintaining strong margins through its enterprise-focused business model (and charging more per models 👀), while acknowledging that other players in the industry (gee, wonder who he’s talking about…) may be overextending themselves financially by betting too aggressively on future revenue growth.

Why this matters: Early leads mean nothing when the technology moves this fast. Google went from “dead on arrival” in AI to forcing OpenAI into emergency mode in just two years.

For anyone building workflows around these tools: don't get locked into one platform. Today's leader could be tomorrow's also-ran. And if Anthropic's IPO actually happens? That'll be the moment we find out if these sky-high valuations are based on real business fundamentals or just hype.

Consider Anthropic’s S-1 filing likely to be one of the most scrutinized in market history… or at least since WeWork and their “community-based EBITDA.“ Good times.

Prompt Tip of the Day

Here's a phrase that'll improve how you use Gemini's Nano Banana image model: “keeping everything else the same.” Sounds obvious, but it works like magic.

How to use it:

  1. Start with a base image: either generate something new or upload one to edit.

  2. List out everything you want to tweak, but tackle them individually, only asking for one change at a time per prompt.

  3. After each request, add the phrase “…keeping everything else the same.” This locks Nano Banana's focus on the detail you're changing while, y’know, preserving everything else.

  4. One change → result. Second change → result. Third change → result. You're iteratively sculpting the image into exactly what you want.

Why this matters: Without this phrase, the model often reinterprets your entire image with each prompt. You then have to wrestle it back to your original vision over and over. This simple addition keeps your edits moving forward instead of sideways.
