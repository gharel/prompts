OpenAI’s DevDay made one thing clear: the “computing interface” of the future will be voice.

OpenAI loves to time its drama around its DevDays. Or maybe it’s the other way around?

Either way, after a week of wild headlines for the soon to be* $150B startup, OpenAI held its DevDay 2024 on Tuesday, and, among many other things, it showed how you can program drones from scratch:

The drone literally streams video of the crowd, live. Here’s more about this.

The big news of the day is the company’s Realtime API, which lets people build products using the new technology that powers Advanced Voice Mode (the new talking ChatGPT that sounds more like a real human).

The real question is: how much does it charge for awkward silence?

Where does this go from here? Eventually, OpenAI will add vision and video. Which leads us to the OTHER big news of the day:

Microsoft announced "Copilot Vision" for Edge, allowing Copilot to “see” web pages on your screen and talk to you about them.

And that’s where this is all going: The end of the keyboard as the dominant way we communicate with computers.

Google has voice AI, Meta has voice AI, Microsoft has voice AI, and soon, every startup with an OpenAI account will have voice AI. It’s like the Oprah meme: You get a voice AI! You get a voice AI! Everyone gets a voice AI!

And once that happens: it’s going to become A LOT more common to talk instead of type.
