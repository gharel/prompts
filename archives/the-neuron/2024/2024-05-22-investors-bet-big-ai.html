The flood of money into AI just doesn’t stop.

It’s raining, it’s pouring. Money into AI is soaring. Investors said big bets ahead. Even big banks aren’t ignoring. 🎶

Investors, institutions, and individuals alike are drooling over what they see as a once-in-a-lifetime AI opportunity. Just this past week was the largest in AI fundraising since Microsoft poured $10B into OpenAI last January.

And it’s not just the ChatGPTs of the world bagging checks; it’s the entire spectrum of AI construction from the ground up.

Starting with infrastructure. Yesterday, CoreWeave raised $7.5B (B = Billion) in debt to rent more AI chips from Nvidia in a deal led by Blackstone.

Then there are enterprise tools, like Scale AI, which helps companies organize and label their data so their AI models excel. They just bagged a cool billion dollars yesterday.

Scale AI

Plus, industry-specific enterprise tools—for literally every industry!

  • PolyAI, a builder of AI voice assistants for call centers, secured $50M.

  • Grasp AI, an AI assistant for investment bankers and consultants, raised $2M.

  • Leya, an AI legal assistant that cites public legal sources accurately, snagged $10.5M.

Leya AI

Then there are consumer AI tools for you and me. 

  • Suno, an AI music generator “where anyone can make music,” announced $125M in funding.

  • Gamma, an AI presentation helper we use to turn docs/outlines into presentation drafts, raised $12M.

And just when you think investors have run out of cash to splash, a French company called “H” with literally 40 words on its website (yes, we counted) raised a $220M seed round (!) to build AGI.

Even Congress wants a slice. Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer recommended that Congress allocate >$32B to developing safe AI over the next three years.

And this all happened in just the past week!

Why it matters: If this isn’t the peak AI cycle, then we’re not sure what is. Ultimately, whether this bubble bursts depends on whether AI truly affects business productivity in the trillions.

Companies are also splurging on AI (e.g. IT teams at big enterprises are spending 2x more on AI than they did last year), but whether it’s paying off is yet to be seen.
