Spotify launches idea → playlist AI feature.

With Spotify’s new AI feature, “AI Playlist,” just describe the type of music you’re looking for, and the AI will spit out a customized playlist.

For instance, we might say, “Give me a playlist for late-night writing sprints,” and the AI will spit out ~30 songs that match our vibe.

If the playlist doesn’t hit our beat, we can tweak it by giving additional instructions, like “Less lyrical songs.”

The feature will initially roll out to Premium subscribers in the UK/Australia—here’s how to find it:

Here’s our take: It’s…..fine???

It might ease the embarrassment for those of us who dread sharing our Spotify playlists (guilty), but it hardly redefines music discovery—Pandora has been on this beat for years.

A reminder: Spotify rolled out a somewhat similar feature last year, the “AI DJ,” which curates songs based on your listening habits.

FYI: AI DJ missed the mark more than a DJ spinning gangster rap at a corporate networking event.

AI music = overrated.

One glaring omission from Spotify's recent AI “innovations”? Music created by AI.

It’s no surprise: artists aren’t exactly fans of AI-generated music—last week, 200 big names signed an open letter to Big Tech, calling for an end to “the assault on human creativity.”

Most music listeners don’t care much for AI music either:

Sure, AI music generators like Suno have their charm, but there’s something about knowing a track wasn’t made by humans that kinda ruins the experience for us.

We’re not saying AI-crafted tunes are all bad—take a look at this insane demo of upcoming tool Udio we stumbled upon this week:

But for AI-generated tunes without lyrics, there’s no reason people shouldn’t just use the massive amount of free stock music and instrumentals out there on the internet.

Why it matters: AI's role in music isn't doomed—there’s probably some other form of this that isn’t just “type some text, get a full song”. We hope it’ll evolve into tools that amplify rather than substitute artistic expression, possibly by granting artists greater creative control over their music.
