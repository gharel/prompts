Reid Hoffman talks with AI Reid Hoffman.

In AI, we’ve seen lots of efforts to recreate people.

  • Character.ai lets you talk to chatbots that mimic celebrities, like Elon Musk or Socrates.

  • ElevenLabs can replicate someone’s voice with sufficient audio samples.

  • DALL·E 3 has been used to generate AI images that look exactly like actual people.

Yesterday, LinkedIn cofounder Reid Hoffman put everything together with a digital replica named “REID AI” that was trained on decades of Reid’s books, speeches, and podcasts to look, talk, and sound just like… HIM!

It. Is. Astonishing.

There were tells that REID's AI twin wasn’t real (random hand gestures, many buzzwords, and worse hair), but unless you’re familiar with Reid’s voice and mannerisms and simply zoned out while watching the AI, you’d struggle to pinpoint it as fake.

REID AI, created by Hour One, showcases the most accurate facial expressions we’ve seen yet, and the REID AI actually does look a lot like Reid IRL.

Why it matters: As with any new AI technology, digital twins bring both positive and negative possibilities. But like Reid (& REID AI), we’re optimists.

  • One cool possibility with REID AI is to generate infinite versions of any speech or lecture Reid has given to suit various audiences (like children, non-technical employees, or Spanish speakers).

  • Or, rather than chatting with a Reid Hoffman bot on character.ai, you could interact with REID AI as if it were a real Zoom call, seeking advice on your startup, the future of humanity, or optimizing your LinkedIn profile (see Reid do this here!).

There are, of course, lots of things that could go wrong with this type of technology, as evidenced by a surge in deceptive AI deepfakes this year.

REID AI asked Reid about preventing misuse, who stressed the importance of establishing clear “rules of the road” for both individuals and public figures to protect their identity, privacy, and reputation.

And then there are the deeper, Black Mirror-type questions about whether AI replicas will keep people “alive” after they die.
