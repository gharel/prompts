ChatGPT Search is near, but Google SGE is already here.

BREAKING: OpenAI is building a search engine that will deliver answers from the internet with source citations.

We covered this on Friday and extended our analysis on Saturday’s podcast (Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube).

TLDR: Expect this product to look very similar to ChatGPT, except it has access to the internet, so it can answer questions like “What’s the weather in SF today?”

This is a pretty big deal cause it’s OpenAI explicitly saying, “We are coming for you, Google Search.”

But it won’t be a walk in the park for two reasons:

  1. Google essentially has a monopoly on search, with 92% market share.

  2. Google’s own AI search engine, SGE, is quite good.

It's so good that we’ve actually begun using Google SGE for almost all of the simple questions we’d typically ask Perplexity, and it shouldn't be hard to see why:

google

perplexity

We highly recommend turning on Google’s AI Search—here’s how:

  1. Go to Google Labs.

  2. Find “Search Powered By Generative AI”.

  3. Toggle On “SGE, generative AI in Search”.

Three key points about SGE:

  • It doesn’t provide AI answers for everything, just the easy stuff.

  • It still makes sh*t up (case in point).

  • It displays its sources more prominently than Perplexity, likely to appease publishers.

Why it matters: Google SGE is good. No, great. And Google can turn it on for its billions of users whenever it wants to; it’s more a matter of economics.

So, OpenAI isn’t just vying with Google Search; it's up against both Google Search AND Google SGE.

Either way, ChatGPT's search feature will be a crucial tool for any ChatGPT power users who don’t want to switch tabs to Google for every search question.

In other news: Remember that mysterious chatbot called gpt2-chatbot that popped up and looked competitive, if not better than ChatGPT-4? Well, it has a cousin named “im-a-good-gpt2-chatbot,” and, indeed, it turns out the rumors were true: these models almost definitely originate from OpenAI.

There are all sorts of theories about what these models are—a fine-tuned ChatGPT-2, OpenAI soft-launching ChatGPT-4.5, or a smaller GPT-4 model that’ll power ChatGPT’s search engine.
