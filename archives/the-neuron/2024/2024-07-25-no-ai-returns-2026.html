Ever heard of the prisoner's dilemma? It's that tricky situation where two people have to decide whether to help or betray each other without knowing what the other will do. You know, like you’re both in a holding cell, and the cops want one of you to rat on the other one? 

That’s Big Tech CEOs and AI spending right now. Let us explain: 

Tech giants and VCs are pouring unprecedented amounts of $$$ into AI.

Alphabet's Q2 earnings report yesterday revealed a 91% surge in capital expenditures to $13.2B, with $2.2B spent on AI development alone.

Despite these substantial investments, the returns weren’t anywhere close to a positive ROI.

One analyst even said we’re unlikely to see “material” AI revenue until 2025 or even 2026. 

An analysis of OpenAI's finances paints a similar picture:

  • Operating costs could reach $8.5B.

  • Annual revenues estimated at $3.5-$4.5B.

  • Projected losses up to $5B this year.

Anthropic's situation is potentially even more dire, reportedly making 1/5 to 1/10 of OpenAI's revenue while burning $2.5B in compute costs alone.

Is this sustainable? 

Zuckerberg and Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai say it doesn’t matter—it’s necessary. Both argued that while their companies might be spending too much today, they're making an informed decision for tomorrow.

And this brings us to the prisoner’s dilemma: do nothing now = risk falling behind the other guy when the payday DOES arrive. 

Why does this matter? AI hype is propping up the tech sector, and by extension, the entire friggin economy. If returns don't materialize soon, we could see a significant market correction. Case in point: Nvidia had a bad three-day stock plunge, and it wiped out more value than 96% of the S&P 500 (y’know, the top 500 biggest companies on the U.S. Stock Market).
