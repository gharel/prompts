Palantir is lowkey crushing it right now…so who are they, and what do they do?

It looks like one of the newest benefactors of the AI boom is Palantir—that’s the trending AI company founded by Alex Karp and Peter Thiel wayyy back in 2003.

While most AI companies were busy building chatbots and image generators, Palantir spent two decades becoming the special forces of enterprise AI.

Their secret sauce? Building platforms that help organizations make sense of massive amounts of data. Here’s what they offer:

  • Gotham helps intelligence agencies connect dots across datasets (it may have helped find Bin Laden).

  • Foundry does the same for businesses, from supply chains to factory operations.

  • Palantir’s new AI Platform integrates various AI models (like Claude) into these systems.

And the headlines have been trending well for Palantir lately, too:

  1. Partnering with Anthropic to bring Claude to intelligence agencies through their ultra-secure IL6 environment (that’s one level below top secret…)

  2. Helping Wendy's automate their $1 Frosty supply chain decisions.

  3. Like we said yesterday, they’ll potentially play a big role in Trump's proposed “AI Manhattan Project.”

Oh yeah, and Palantir’s recent earnings call went well, with Q3 earnings up 30% year over year due to demand for its commercial business—according to their CEO, “we absolutely eviscerated this quarter.”

Here’s the thing, though—it ain’t all roses. Financial outlets are saying the company is overvalued big time right now. They’ll need to maintain growth while reaching consistent profitability, plus navigate privacy concerns and criticism over their ties to the military.
