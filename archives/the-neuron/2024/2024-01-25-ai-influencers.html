AI influencers are becoming a real marketing strategy for brands. 

See these three women?

They’re social media influencers that boast millions of combined followers and charge some brands 6-figure fees for brand promotions.

They’re also not real people…

As AI-generated images become increasingly lifelike (cheers, Midjourney), more brands are choosing AI-generated "influencers" over flesh-and-blood humans.

The primary driver? You guessed it: money. 

Take H&M, for instance: they slashed costs by 91% per person using "virtual creator content" in their marketing mix.

And here’s what the creator of one AI influencer had to say:

“We were taken aback by the skyrocketing rates influencers charge nowadays. That got us thinking, ‘What if we just create our own influencer?’ The rest is history. We unintentionally created a monster. A beautiful one, though.”

Diana Núñez, co-founder of the Barcelona-based agency The Clueless, which created Aitana.

Why it matters: Now you might be thinking: “Noah, Pete, isn’t this hyper-sexualization of non-existent women driving consumerism a bad thing?”

Probably.

Yet, as many Redditors note, plenty of human influencers promote equally superficial and unrealistic lifestyles, so, in a way, AI influencers are just a new twist on an old theme.
