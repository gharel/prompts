OpenAI wants Axel Springer for its precious data archive.

ZDNET

Well, well, well, how the turntables.

Just five months back, Axel Springer was rumored to be joining a coalition planning to sue companies like OpenAI for stealing their content.

Now, it’s teaming up with OpenAI to put news from outlets like Politico, Bild, and, yes, Business Insider right into ChatGPT.

That’s assuming people actually want to read news in ChatGPT:

  • Our trials with ChatGPT, Google SGE, and Perplexity for news have been so-so—we’ve found it hard to find exactly what you’re looking for.

  • Plus, people seek out outlets they like…and both the political left and right are already fuming about Axel Springer being too biased (LOL).

TBT: Big Tech has tried to aggregate news before…with little success (e.g. Facebook News).

Here’s our take: This is all about OpenAI getting their hands on more data.

For ChatGPT to improve, it needs more unique data, a Herculean task when you’ve practically devoured the entire internet. This is why OpenAI paid the American Journalism Project $5M in July for news access.

It’s also why companies like Reddit and Twitter are paywalling their treasure troves of data so chatbots can’t get their paws on it.

Data is the new oil, folks!
