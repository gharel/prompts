AGI: Overrated OR Underrated?!

Human Progress, from WaitButWhy

The builders of a new technology think it will either create a utopia or destroy the world. No biggie.

Enter AGI — Artificial General Intelligence.

  • Current AI has specialized intelligence, meaning it’s great at singular tasks like copywriting.

  • AGI has general intelligence, meaning it can do just about anything.

Like anything, anything: picture a computer that can learn, think, reason, decide, refine, and take action... and it doesn't need a babysitter.

The fear is that AGI could go rogue and we’d be utterly helpless. Think HAL 9000 from Space Odyssey, but even worse.

It’s getting so real that AI vets like Geoffrey Hinton and Sam Altman signed a letter warning “of extinction from AI on scale with nuclear war”, and others like Elon Musk signed a petition to put AI development in a timeout.

Pause. Take a deep breath. Okay. Proceed.

It’s not all gloom-and-doom. Nvidia reassured the Senate that “uncontrollable AGI … is science fiction”, and most of our tech-y friends agree. Many are even betting that a superintelligent AI could usher in a new era of productivity and affluence.

Here’s our take: We’re cautious optimists. AI accelerates exponentially, but the baby AGI we see today (aka autonomous agents) are quite clunky.

Either way, AI should be taken very seriously, and the Senate’s AI summit is a step in the right direction.

As for you all? No need to worry about AGI yet. Go touch grass, have a beer, and shoot a sweet message to your parents. :)

Morgan Stanley Trained A Chatbot On All Its Knowledge.

Morgan Stanley’s just released an internal AI assistant trained on ~100,000 of its research reports and documents.

The result: an AMA on everything finance-related, from market trends to legal processes. Huge. Time. Saver.

McKinsey pulled a similar move last month, arming employees with a chatbot for anything consulting-related: research, data, past cases, & more.

The whole train-a-chatbot-on-my-company-knowledge is something we expect a lot of companies to do soon.
