Canva Brings Gifts 🎁

If there's been a pattern in AI recently, it's that competitors like to release things the same week as each other.

Today, that's Canva, following Adobe's AI drop earlier this week. Canva's 125 million users will be getting:

  • Text-to-slide deck

  • Erase and replace items in an image

  • Image-to-design template (e.g., auto-creating a fully-designed flyer using an image)

  • More AI-generated text, now supporting 18 languages

  • Better and faster text-to-image

Canva users can check out their homepage and unwrap these features from the gift boxes.

The announcement clearly puts Canva side-by-side with Adobe. Still, "I think it’s really important just to focus on our own race", says Canva CEO Melanie Perkins.

Zoom out: In just two weeks, we had Microsoft Office/Teams, Google Workspace, Adobe and Canva announce more AI. That's on top of Slack, Discord, Salesforce and more. It only took 4 months for tech to shove AI everywhere we use software.

GPT-4 is General Intelligence? 🧠

Whenever an AI gets too smart, chatter immediately turns to artificial general intelligence (AGI), one of the holy grails of AI.

What it means: AGI is when an AI model can do any intellectual task that a human can. That's in contrast to the type of AI that runs your social media feeds: narrow, specific, single use case.

A team at Microsoft believes GPT-4 is an early signal of AGI.

Their core argument: GPT-4 is simply so good at so many things - language tone/style, understanding complex ideas, code, drawing pictures using code, math, using tools - that it must be a step towards AGI.

It's not AGI itself. There's still a large gap between GPT-4 and humans, along with abilities like "learning from experience" that GPT-4 doesn't do at all.

But you can't help but think about GPT-5. Reports claim OpenAI is using GPT-4 to train GPT-5. How much closer to AGI will GPT-5 be?
