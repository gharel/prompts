Inside OpenAI’s Search For New Data 🥜

Midjourney’s interpretation of this story…

AI models need tons of data. It’s like gasoline for a car.

This is why OpenAI has feasted on the entire internet — the more it learns, the sharper its answers.

But what happens once OpenAI turns smarter than the internet itself?

A tempting strategy was to “borrow” data from content-rich playgrounds like Twitter and Reddit. But those firms quickly wised up and said, “Nuh-uh. Pay up.”

Now, OpenAI is courting individual companies for their data. Just this week, it forged one-to-one partnerships with:

  • The Associated Press, tapping into its news archives dating back to 1985.

  • Shutterstock, accessing its repository of images, videos, music and metadata.

In return, these sites will enjoy “priority access” to OpenAI’s latest technology.

Why it matters: Niche data like Reddit forums or Shutterstock images can help AI models get just a little bit better. The race to create the best models might increasingly become a contest who can afford the most “new” data.

Bard Is Getting Better … BUT It’s Still Not Great 📈📉

ChatGPT is 16x more popular than Bard by web traffic. Why?

It’s simply better at almost everything: writing, coding, & reasoning.

The only time Bard seems to take the lead is when it comes to current events, like “summarize the latest AI regulation.”

Yesterday, though, Bard announced some intriguing new features:

  • you can listen to responses (in 40+ languages).

  • ability to export code to Replit.

  • use images in prompts.

Here’s our take: Bard still isn’t giving nearly as good of responses as ChatGPT-4. Bard needs to get better + fully integrate with Google Workspace apps like Docs and Gmail. Then we’ll talk.

For now, Bard's relationship with ChatGPT mirrors that of Scottie Pippen to Michael Jordan — forever overshadowed.
