GPT-4 Is Here. Here's What You Need To Know👇

OpenAI launched GPT-4 yesterday, and it could be revolutionary.

Here's everything you need to know:

1/ It's way, way smarter: GPT-4's reasoning skills and ability to handle complex tasks are far superior. The new model performs in the 90th percentile on the Bar Exam, while GPT-3.5 performed in the 10th percentile.

2/ Its visual skills are extraordinary: GPT-4 lets ChatGPT see and understand images. The model can apply logic to pictures, like describing, analyzing, and performing tasks on them. That enabled a pretty insane "napkin sketch to website" demo.

3/ You control its style: ChatGPT-3 writes like a robot (shocker!). GPT-4 introduces a massive upgrade to ChatGPT's 'steerability,' or the ability to steer the model's style in a direction you prefer. Plus, it can handle 25,000+ words.

4/ It behaves better: Remember when Reddit users broke ChatGPT to say crazy stuff? GPT-4 responds to 'disallowed' content 82% less often than GPT-3.5.

GPT-4 is already supercharging companies, supporting Khan Academy to build an assistant to support students and teachers and Be My Eyes to create a virtual AI assistant that aids low-vision and blind users.

So far, only ChatGPT Plus subscribers have been granted access to GPT-4, and its API will welcome developers over the coming weeks.

Already, people are discovering some pretty crazy capabilities: it can code games, write lawsuits and even lie to people.

Bottom line: A wave of new features, products, and companies are about to emerge from GPT-4. Get ready!

Google & Anthropic Make Announcements 🚀

Both Google and Anthropic released significant announcements about their AI-powered tools for businesses yesterday.

But with the GPT-4 excitement, their finer details might've gotten lost in your feed.

First, Google opened dev access to its PaLM API, which lets people build content and chat applications on top of Google's best language models. Did someone say emergency PaLM hackathon? 🤲

Then, Anthropic took its own swing at OpenAI by opening access to Claude, its highly developed ChatGPT competitor. According to the team, Claude is like ChatGPT but more conversational and interactive. ChatGPT-3, that is.

Finally, Google Workspace got in on the action with new AI features that make drafting content and emails seamless in Docs and Gmail for its over 3 billion users.

Bottom line: OpenAI's competitors are shipping at a rapid pace, but those who jump the gun in the news risk shooting themselves in the foot.
