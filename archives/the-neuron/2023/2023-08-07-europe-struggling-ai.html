Europe’s AI Landscape (& Why It’s A Problem) 🇪🇺

The US is miles ahead of Europe in the AI game. More startups. More funding.

Europe lagging in tech isn’t anything new … but for AI, it could spell real trouble.

See, the EU doesn’t want its companies using AI models that break their rules. And guess what: not one American foundational model (like GPT, LLaMA, etc) passes the EU AI Act’s rules. 

So what’s the solution here?

European AI companies that stick to the EU’s playbook. Yet, no standout contenders have emerged just yet. Your leading competitors:

  • Aleph Alpha, that German startup that is still training its ChatGPT competitor.

  • Mistral AI, a Paris-based startup that bagged $113 million in funding for a website with only 31 words. Seriously.

Why it matters: The EU AI Act will probably pass. This could be a huge opportunity for European AI companies to step up and offer services that align with privacy rules and offer companies control over their data.

Tell, Don’t Show AI How To Do Something ✋

We dug through a 10,000-word research paper and here’s what we found:

  1. AI was told to learn 6 new skills.

  2. In group #1, an AI was handed "instruction manuals" to pick up new skills.

  3. In group #2, an AI was presented with "examples" to follow.

It’s the age-old “tell me how” vs. “show me how.”

RESULT: AI gets the hang of things faster when you “tell it” how, rather than when you “show it” how.

What this matters (for you): Whenever using AI at work, offer it context on how to do something rather than just feeding it examples. This might include:

  • API or library documentation.

  • User guides for in-house tools like Salesforce.

  • Company rulebooks (policies, regulations).

If your document exceeds ChatGPT’s input limits, try to throw some of it in custom instructions or opt for Claude which offers way longer context windows.
