Google & Microsoft are playing tag 🗨

Only three things are certain in life: birth, death, and Google blitzing search engine competitors.

Now, ChatGPT is on the receiving end of Google's strike. Yesterday, Google CEO Sundar Pichai revealed Bard, a ChatGPT rival. Google also scheduled a last-minute event for Wednesday, Feb 8th.

Beyond the poor name choice, Bard is powered by Google's large language model LaMDA and will be distributed to "trusted testers" over the coming weeks.

  • At first glance, Bard appears to possess elements of both ChatGPT and Google Search, aggregating web-based information into high-quality, brief answers.

  • Bard can answer questions about recent events. That's been a big issue for ChatGPT, whose knowledge ends at 2021.

Soon, you’ll see AI-powered features in Search that distill complex information and multiple perspectives into easy-to-digest formats, so you can quickly understand the big picture and learn more from the web.

Sundar Pichai, Google CEO

Like any older sibling would, Microsoft spat back only minutes after Google's announcement to advertise its own "major news event" today, February 7th.

The event is expected to publicize ChatGPT's integration into Bing, among other significant partnerships.

Bottom line: Things are moving quickly, and the Google homepage you've had for 20 years might change forever pretty soon.

Let's just hope we don't have to say, "Let me Bard/Bing that real quick," anytime soon.

Cohere seeking $6 billion valuation

It feels like crypto summer all over again, except this time tech unicorns have '.ai' attached to their websites instead of '.eth'.

One such company, Cohere, is in talks to raise hundreds of millions of dollars at an over $6 billion valuation.

  • Unlike ChatGPT, Cohere is focused on supporting "business-applicable tasks like answering questions than writing poems." In other words, businesses > us average folk.

Zoom out: It's a big number attached to a small startup, but never say never to a tech startup co-founded by former Google researchers.

(plus, why the knock on poems? If you're better than ChatGPT, go out and show 'em).
