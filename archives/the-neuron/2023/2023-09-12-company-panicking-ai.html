5 Ways Companies Can Foster Collaboration Around AI At Work.

Here’s the AI scene at most companies right now: senior leadership is privately discussing how AI will transform business, and employees are secretly using AI for their tasks.

There is zero collaboration. Zero alignment. And, most critically, zero trust.

This needs to change. We’ve chatted with a couple big companies about how they’re positioning AI at the heart of how they operate.

Here are 5 ideas to foster AI collaboration in the office:

  1. AI prompt library: create a place where employees can post and discuss prompts they’re using in their workflows. A simple GDoc will do.

  2. AI-centric hackathon: organize an event where teams brainstorm and build AI solutions for company processes.

  3. AI idea box: introduce a suggestion box (online or IRL) where employees can drop ideas for AI solutions.

  4. ChatGPT Plus on the house: Reimburse ChatGPT Plus for everyone. But always have security guidelines in place!

  5. Get the convo going: motivate the sharing of AI resources, guides, and articles on your Slack or Teams. This starts top-down from senior leadership.

Our Honest Review Of Google Workspace’s Duet AI.

Google has been hyping up it’s “Duet AI” for a while now.

The end goal: arm every user with an AI assistant for all your work apps (Docs, Sheets, Meet, Slides, & Gmail).

We signed up for the trial (normally $30/user/month) to see if it lives up to the hype.

tl;dr: Duet AI is not great yet. Wait to buy. We’ll tell you when.

Today, you only get the basic features like drafting emails, organizing spreadsheets, and generating images. ChatGPT + Midjourney do all of this, but better.

Duet AI becomes worth it when it can execute tasks that involve all of your company’s context, not just one doc.

Picture this:

  1. Takes notes on CEO feedback to Q4 priorities from this Meet.

  2. Update the Q4 strategy memo in Docs based on these notes.

  3. Create 10 pages in Slides based on the strategy doc.

The demos hint these features are coming, but it’s unclear when. For now, your $30 is better spent on an office keg or feline.
