The New Weird Thing The Internet Loves 📸

Nothing, Forever.

It's a Twitch channel that live streams a 3D cartoon rip-off of Seinfeld, complete with laugh tracks and classic jokes. The catch? An AI is generating every piece of it.

Soup to nuts. With just a team of two, the company behind it built the whole production to generate nearly everything, from dialogue to speech to the camera direction, using machine learning.

Our take. We tuned into Nothing, Forever for longer than we'd care to admit. It was weird. It knew how everything should be - the joke style, how the scenes cut, the pacing - but what we saw and heard was somewhere between awkward and straight up bad.

But hey, freaking AI models made all of this. We definitely wouldn't do any better! Plus, it kinda got better the longer we watched it? Didn't hurt that 10,000(!) others were tuned in with us.

Fast and faster. Just a few weeks ago, we nodded along to a tweet saying the future would be filled with endless, AI-generated content.

Nothing, Forever isn't perfect, but it proved that anyone tweeting a prediction about AI should be ready to see it come to life a lot faster than they were expecting.

That's 9 Digits for ChatGPT 🚀

That was insanely fast.

Outside analysis says ChatGPT hit 100 million monthly active users in January, just two months after launch.

Compare that to these other apps:

  • TikTok: 9 months

  • Instagram: 2.5 years

  • Spotify: 6 years

  • Facebook: 4.5 years

No surprise here. When everyone you can think of - students, CEOs, small business owners - has tried it, part of us wonders if 100 million is underestimating it.

Pulse Check, AI Startups 🏥

Time to think bigger.

Some startup teams started the year thinking, "If we build AI-powered [legal/support/writing] assistants fast enough, we could disrupt the incumbents."

Well, that's now proving tough when the incumbents decide to build the same thing that you're building:

  • "Let's build AI for customer support!" → Intercom releases their own AI features

  • "How about AI for legal?" → Ironclad announces AI features

  • "Ok, an AI meeting assistant?" → Microsoft Teams turns on its AI features

That's just in the last two days.

Tough times. Can't they let the new kids play?

PS: That Microsoft Teams announcement is the OpenAI partnership at its best. Microsoft gets a head start on anything from OpenAI, which lets it release new products as fast as a startup would.

Just take GPT-4. Everyone knows it's around the corner, but Microsoft already has its hands on it.
