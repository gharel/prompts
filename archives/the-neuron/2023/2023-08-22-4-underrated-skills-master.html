AI Job Openings Have Skyrocketed And So Have Their Salaries.

Some folks think AI is overhyped.

This may be true regarding mainstream usage. Search interest for ChatGPT dropped in BOTH July and August.

However, businesses certainly don’t think AI is overhyped at all. Here's how we know: the demand for AI talent is greater than ever (by far):

And companies are willing to pay handsomely for those who really know their sh*t (a recent Netflix AI job went viral for its $900k salary tag).

Interestingly, the number of people identifying as “AI talent” has also skyrocketed.

A LinkedIn report found that users added AI-related terms like “ChatGPT” and “Prompt Engineering” to their profiles 15x more in June than in January.

But as more professionals label themselves as "AI talent," companies will have to determine which "AI skills" are genuinely relevant.

Here’s our take: Hard skills like AI Engineering (think: you can actually build AI systems and products) are underhyped. Soft skills like being an above-average prompt engineer are overhyped.

Being “Human-Like” Is More Important Than Ever.

LinkedIn

As AI skills grow in importance, so too are people skills.

The same LinkedIn AI report found that since ChatGPT emerged, the fastest growing in-demand skills are actually anti-AI skills…

  • Flexibility (+158%).

  • Professional ethics (+120%).

  • Social perceptiveness (+118%).

  • Self-management (83%).

…and an astonishing 92% of execs agree that people skills are more crucial now than ever before.

What this means for you: In a world where AI can handle many human tasks, excelling at what AI can't do (at least for the foreseeable future) is a tremendous advantage.

For us, that's crafting content that resonates as human-written, not AI-generated (cause it is). It's making typos in our spellng. Cracking jokes. Embracing a cat-themed brand.

This piece does a great job explaining the importance of distinguishing oneself against generative AI, using writing as an example.
